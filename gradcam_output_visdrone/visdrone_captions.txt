Short 

Dense VisDrone scene — many small objects are detected and the heatmap is sharply focused on them.

Long 
This VisDrone example shows a crowded aerial scene with several small objects such as vehicles and pedestrians. Our baseline VisDrone model detects most of these instances, and the prediction panel in the middle is filled with bounding boxes and reasonable confidence scores. In the heatmap, activation is concentrated exactly over these predicted objects and remains low in the empty road regions. This indicates that the network’s attention is aligned with the true targets rather than random background, which is the behaviour we expect from a good small-object detector.

Short 

Robust to scale and perspective — small objects at different depths are detected and highlighted.

Long:

This VisDrone panel contains objects at different scales and at an oblique viewing angle, which is typical for UAV footage. The prediction image shows that the baseline model successfully detects both closer and farther objects. In the heatmap, we see multiple “hot spots” exactly over these instances, while the rest of the scene receives only weak activation. This demonstrates that the multi-scale design of YOLO, combined with VisDrone-style training, helps the network remain robust to scale and perspective changes for small objects.

Cluttered urban scene where the model still focuses on true objects instead of background.

Long:

In this cluttered urban environment there are many potential distractors such as buildings, road markings and textured background regions. The prediction panel shows that the detector concentrates its boxes on meaningful objects, such as cars and pedestrians. Difficult lighting case where the model still preserves reasonable attention on key objects.

Long:

This frame represents a more challenging VisDrone condition, for example low contrast or evening lighting. Despite the difficulty, the prediction image shows that the model is still able to detect a subset of the important objects in the scene.
